## 1. INTRODUCTION AND BACKGROUND

The 119 Road Construction Company (RCC), GREF, Border Roads Organisation (BRO) is responsible for the up-gradation and maintenance of the Kimin–Potin Road in Arunachal Pradesh — a strategically important corridor connecting Potin in Arunachal Pradesh with North Lakhimpur in Assam. In connection with the proposed up-gradation of this road to higher specifications, four major permanent bridges along this route require detailed structural evaluation to assess their adequacy for present-day and projected traffic loads.

These bridges, all constructed in 1968, have been in service for approximately **58 years**. While a preliminary inspection using a Mobile Bridge Inspection Unit has not revealed major visible structural distress, noticeable vibrations under heavy vehicular loading have been recorded. Furthermore, the original design drawings and load calculations are no longer available, necessitating a comprehensive scientific assessment of their current structural condition, load-carrying capacity, and remaining service life.

IIT Patna, through its Department of Civil and Environmental Engineering, has well-established expertise in structural assessment, non-destructive testing (NDT), and numerical modelling of civil infrastructure. We are pleased to submit this Techno-Commercial Proposal in response to the request dated 01 Apr 2026.

## 3. SCOPE OF WORK

The proposed scope covers the following activities across all four bridges:

1. **Detailed Visual Inspection** — component-wise condition rating per IRC:SP:35 / IRC:SP:18-2022
2. **Non-Destructive Testing (NDT)** — concrete strength, rebar mapping, carbonation depth
3. **Structural Load Testing** — static and dynamic load tests
4. **Structural Modelling and Analysis** — 3D FEM model calibrated against field data
5. **Capacity Assessment** — current load-carrying capacity under IRC Class 70R and AA loading
6. **Remaining Service Life Estimation**
7. **Strengthening Recommendations** — with conceptual designs and cost estimates
8. **Comprehensive Audit Reports** per IRC and IS codes

## 4. TECHNICAL METHODOLOGY

### Phase 1: Desk Study and Pre-Inspection Preparation (Week 1–2)

- Review of available records: maintenance logs, BRO inspection reports, Google Earth imagery
- Review of applicable codes: IRC:6-2017 (Loads), IRC:112-2020 (Concrete Bridges), IRC:SP:35-1990, IS 456:2000, IRC:SP:18-2022 (Bridge Inspection Manual)
- Preparation of detailed inspection checklist tailored to each bridge type
- Identification of NDT equipment requirements and logistical planning for field deployment
- Safety plan preparation for remote site operations

### Phase 2: Detailed Field Inspection (Week 3–5)

**2.1 Visual and Physical Inspection**

A trained multi-disciplinary team will conduct systematic visual inspection of all structural elements:
- **Superstructure:** Beams (T-beams, girders), deck slab, wearing course, expansion joints, bearings
- **Substructure:** Abutments, piers, pile caps/foundations (as accessible)
- **Appurtenances:** Parapets, crash barriers, drainage spouts, approach slabs

Condition will be rated on a 0–5 scale per IRC:SP:18-2022. Photographic documentation will be comprehensive and georeferenced.

**2.2 Non-Destructive Testing (NDT)**

The following NDT investigations will be conducted at statistically representative locations on each bridge:

| Test | Equipment | Purpose |
|------|-----------|---------|
| Rebound Hammer Test (IS 13311-2) | Schmidt Hammer N-type | Indicative surface concrete strength |
| Ultrasonic Pulse Velocity (UPV) Test (IS 13311-1) | PUNDIT Plus | Concrete quality, void/crack detection |
| Half-Cell Potential (ASTM C876) | Canin+ / Proceq | Probability of active rebar corrosion |
| Cover Meter Survey | Profometer 650 | Rebar cover and spacing mapping |
| Core Extraction and Testing (IS 516) | Core cutting machine | In-situ compressive strength (fck) |
| Carbonation Depth Test (Phenolphthalein) | — | Durability and corrosion front |
| Chloride Content Analysis | Lab analysis | Corrosion risk in aggressive environment |
| Ground Penetrating Radar (GPR) (optional, if accessible) | GSSI SIR 3000 | Delamination, voids, rebar mapping |

Minimum 3 cores per bridge will be extracted from critical members (beams, deck slab, piers) for laboratory compressive strength testing.

**2.3 Dynamic/Vibration Measurement**

Given the reported vibrations under heavy vehicular loading:
- Ambient vibration measurements using tri-axial accelerometers (MEMS-based)
- Identification of natural frequencies, mode shapes, and damping ratios (Operational Modal Analysis)
- These will serve as calibration data for the FEM model

### Phase 3: Structural Modelling and Analysis (Week 6–8)

**3.1 As-Built Geometry Reconstruction**

- Physical measurement of all structural dimensions (cross-sections, spans, bearing locations)
- Reconstruction of as-built structural drawings using field measurements and available type drawings from BRO/IRC standards applicable to 1968-era construction

**3.2 Finite Element Model Development**

- 3D FEM models developed in SAP2000 / ANSYS / ETABS for each bridge
- Material properties assigned based on NDT results (in-situ fck, modulus of elasticity)
- Boundary conditions representative of actual field conditions
- Model validation/calibration against measured natural frequencies from dynamic testing

**3.3 Load Cases and Analysis**

Analysis will be conducted for the following load combinations per IRC:6-2017:
- Dead Load (DL) + SIDL
- IRC Class 70R (tracked and wheeled) — original design load
- IRC Class AA Loading
- IRC Class A (for comparison and fatigue evaluation)
- Braking force, wind load (Zone IV / terrain category), seismic load (Zone V – Arunachal Pradesh, IS 1893)
- Temperature effects (gradient and uniform)

**3.4 Capacity Evaluation**

- Flexural and shear capacity of beams and deck slab per IRC:112-2020 / IS 456:2000
- Comparison of demand (from analysis) vs. capacity (from material testing)
- Identification of critical deficient members
- Assessment of fatigue damage accumulated over 58 years of service

### Phase 4: Remaining Service Life Estimation (Week 8–9)

- Corrosion propagation modelling using Fick's second law of diffusion (chloride ingress model)
- Carbonation-induced dealkalization front progression model
- Estimation of remaining service life under current traffic and under proposed enhanced loading
- Presentation as a probabilistic assessment with confidence bounds

### Phase 5: Strengthening Recommendations (Week 9–11)

Based on the condition assessment and capacity evaluation, appropriate strengthening interventions will be recommended. Strategies to be evaluated include:

| Deficiency | Possible Intervention Strategy |
|------------|-------------------------------|
| Insufficient flexural capacity | CFRP/GFRP laminate bonding to soffit of beams (fib Bulletin 14) |
| Insufficient shear capacity | CFRP U-wrap / near-surface mounted (NSM) CFRP rods |
| Corrosion-damaged rebars | Concrete patch repair + cathodic protection / zinc mesh anode |
| Deteriorated bearings | Bearing replacement with elastomeric/POT bearings |
| Expansion joint failure | Modular expansion joint replacement |
| Substructure scour/damage | Guniting, micro-piling, scour protection |
| Deck delamination | Hydro-demolition + overlay with silica fume concrete |

For each bridge, two to three technically feasible strengthening alternatives will be presented with:
- Conceptual structural drawings
- Bill of quantities (BOQ) and indicative cost estimate
- Recommended priority and phasing

### Phase 6: Reporting (Week 11–13)

A comprehensive report will be prepared for each bridge, followed by an integrated summary report. Report structure:

1. Executive Summary
2. Bridge Inventory
3. Inspection Findings (with photo log, defect maps)
4. NDT Results and Interpretation
5. Structural Analysis Results
6. Capacity Assessment and Load Rating
7. Remaining Service Life
8. Strengthening Recommendations
9. Maintenance Plan (short-term, medium-term)
10. Appendices: Raw data, drawings, calculations
